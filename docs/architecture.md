# Architecture

The simulation and procedural renderer use web standards without runtime library dependencies. Electron wraps the same HTML entry point with sandboxing, context isolation, disabled Node integration, and external navigation blocked.

- `src/simulation.js`: seeded 192 × 128 world, towns, jobs, research, armies, trade, ecology, tools, and save validation.
- `src/creatures.js`: deterministic identities, personality traits, inherited aptitudes, XP, levels, health capacity, strength, intelligence, and aging.
- `src/daily_life.js` and `src/daily_life_art.js`: saved civilian work, delivery, rest, and social routines with finite carried supplies and close zoom action sprites.
- `src/society.js`: bounded relationships, adult partnership and parent selection, inherited family and cultural identity, four traditions, five ruler personalities, and import validation.
- `src/health.js`: actual creature damage accounting with eight bounded recent records and current health risk detection.
- `src/medicine.js` and `src/medical_art.js`: clinics, hospitals, adult staffing, finite herb gathering, triage, recovery travel, supply consumption, and original medical sprites.
- `src/civic.js` and `src/civic_art.js`: population-based farm/reserve goals, finite granary transfers, on-site schooling, nearby sanctuary rest, wells and original civic sprites.
- `src/crews.js`: unique adult machine crews, boarding intentions and synchronized aboard positions. Crews keep their citizen identity and ordinary nutrition, infection and aging accounting while suspended from other employment.
- `src/pathfinding.js`: bounded A* over walkable tiles. Pure next-waypoint caches are transient; worker intentions are saved so restored worlds evolve identically.
- `src/strategy.js`: supply and army assessments for recovery, construction, defense, expansion, and diplomacy.
- `src/settlements.js`: material-gated construction, crop growth and harvests, windmill bonuses, armories, and training camps.
- `src/infrastructure.js`: timber-funded dirt, stone, and paved roads; finite farm-surplus collection and delivery by carts, steam wagons, and trucks.
- `src/mythic.js`: flying angel guardians and hostile fire-spreading demons.
- `src/heraldry.js`: stable kingdom flag designs and ruler succession.
- `src/world_art.js`: original trees, weapons, vehicles, and landmark canvas sprites.
- `src/defenses.js`: tower fire, castle protection, wounded-soldier capture, prison feeding, timed release, and peace release.
- `src/machines.js`: bounded fleets of water boats, ground artillery, survey balloons, courier aircraft, and larger flying transports.
- `src/machine_art.js`: original vessel, aircraft, cannon, and fortification sprites.
- `src/weather.js` and `src/weather_art.js`: bounded saved rain/storm/drought/blizzard/tsunami events, optional natural scheduling, coastal reach checks, actual crop/soil/health/structure effects and original visual effects.
- `src/sandbox.css`: map-first Observe layout, contextual tool palette and compact dock; summary-first inspectors preserve expanded detail state.
- `src/daylight.js`: a presentation clock independent of calendar years, smooth lighting, and a minimum three-minute cycle at high speed.
- `src/renderer.js`: cached pixel terrain, creatures, town industries, lighting, effects, zoom, and labels.
- `src/app.js`: menu, map preview, input, fixed simulation steps, inspection and camera following, lighting controls, and manual save/export/import.
- `src/layout.css`: a collapsible creator sidebar, compact bottom catalog, and map sized to the remaining space; compact windows preserve zoom and save/load actions.
- `src/desktop.cjs`: Electron window and native application menu.
- `scripts/smoke.cjs`: isolated Electron UI checks using native pointer input, imports, and screenshots in ignored `artifacts/`.

World setup previews the same generated terrain used by the playable world. New worlds begin paused. Twenty simulation seconds make a sandbox year; challenge years take two seconds. XP and aging use that calendar. Elder decline begins after 85 years for people and 50 years for wildlife. Population caps remain 600 people in sandbox or 1,200 in the challenge, 350 animals, 120 kingdoms, 240 colonies, 40 voyages, 60 concurrent trade shipments, 120 local transport vehicles, and 160 boats, artillery pieces, and aircraft.

Biome IDs extend the original terrain IDs so old saves remain readable. Loading older creatures adds deterministic names, traits, and lifetime survival XP while preserving actual health. Validation checks health against derived capacity, limits experience and family records, and validates buildings, worker intentions, lighting settings, roads, transport cargo, stardust, and finite mining strata.

Hills, mountains, and crystal wilds hold finite ore. Population and age increase prospecting range; Iron Age miners can uncover at most three deterministic seams per rocky tile. Depletion is saved. New births fill underrepresented jobs; towns retrain essential professions when none remain. Research requires materials for a first discovery; later kingdoms can recover historical knowledge, and rebels inherit their parent's technology. Forging consumes ore and timber, keeps a bounded spare weapon stock, equips adults, and preserves timber for eligible construction during peace. Armor production can continue when spare weapons are full. Farms produce food, windmills improve harvests, armories accelerate forging, and nearby soldiers earn XP at training camps. Construction consumes timber; buildings can be destroyed by terrain changes and powers. Fourteen ages include material and institution gates through Cosmic Dust. Stardust is ore ID 5, also collected per calendar year by cosmic institutions; old saves receive an empty stardust stock. Universities and monuments multiply research; workshops and factories accelerate gear production. Crossbows and firearms use range and reload intervals; projectiles are visual effects after resolved damage.

Road grades live in a validated tile array, guide A* costs, and improve movement speed. Farm surplus is capped at 120 food per farm. Vehicles remove cargo from farm stock at pickup and add it to kingdom food only at delivery. Vehicles, cargo, destinations, build timers, and ruler IDs survive saves. Hover carriers use bounded land routing; separately modeled aircraft fly directly over water and land. Boats remain on water. Aircraft carry food in persistent cargo, deliver only to peaceful living recipients, and return cargo when a route becomes hostile. Prisoners retain their original identity and kingdom but stop working, fighting, and contributing to sieges while detained. Old saves gain empty infrastructure fields. Flag designs derive from the kingdom ID and color, so succession does not change the flag.

Kingdom decisions consider supplies and army strength. Workers seek food or timber, soldiers share land targets and can retreat, and A* routes avoid impassable tiles. Route searches are bounded to control cost. Ships carry settlers and actual trade cargo; naval combat is not implemented. Traits affect health growth, strength, learning, work XP, or retreat behavior. Citizens begin as adults; newborns grow through an 18-year childhood near home before taking a full job or becoming soldiers. Civilian routines retain finite cargo, energy, a destination, and a phase in saves. Farmers collect only existing farm stock; forage consumes finite local fertility, and timber harvest also reduces fertility. A routine credits carried goods only on return home, while the established kingdom-level mining, smithing, building, and research systems continue their existing aggregate accounting. Kin selection excludes the same family and known shared parents, but it is not a full multigenerational genealogy model. Citizens retain up to eight relationship snapshots; ruler personalities and fixed kingdom cultures modestly change simulation rates and autonomous diplomacy.

The lighting clock advances only while the visible game is running and pauses with the game. It supports 10 minutes, 24 minutes, or 24 real hours at 1×. Speed acceleration is capped so a lighting cycle never takes less than three real minutes. Its phase and duration are saved with the world. Lighting is visual and does not drive creature aging.

Medical buildings reserve one doctor per clinic or two per hospital while retaining an adult farmer and timber worker. Doctors collect finite herbs from nearby fertile terrain and bring carried medicine back to their assigned site. A present healthy doctor treats two clinic patients or three hospital patients at a time, prioritizing the lowest health fraction. Treatment consumes medicine and kingdom food, heals wounds, and reduces infection. Below 60% health, or while infected, citizens interrupt ordinary work and soldiers interrupt combat/training to seek a staffed medical building; they resume at 85% health once infection clears. Towns without a reachable clinic provide slower rest at home. During famine, citizens suffering starvation resume gathering rather than wait indefinitely for foodless treatment. Elder recovery weakens with age and ongoing aging damage remains. Saved medical cargo, recovery state, stock, treatment counts, and damage history are validated; staffing and routing caches are rebuilt each step. Prior damage in legacy worlds is unknown.

Build scripts use `--publish never` so version tags cannot accidentally request publishing credentials. GitHub Actions builds each platform independently and uploads artifacts; public releases are managed separately. See [validation](validation.md) and the ordered [roadmap](roadmap.md).

Basic shelter deficits are funded before construction. Farms target one per 20 residents, up to 32 fields within the 64-building kingdom bound. A field needs an available adult farmer; each completed crop produces 120 food, or 160 with a mill, with a 10% nearby-well bonus. At most 12 units of that same crop enter finite field surplus for collection, with the remainder entering shared harvest stores. Granaries transfer up to 120 existing food into reserve and release it during shortages. Fields use the established aggregate settlement economy; every grain is not individually carried by a visible worker. New civilians temporarily favor farming during shortages, and births, recruitment, optional housing and optional construction wait for reserves. Schools consume a small food allowance while children learn on site. Sanctuaries improve nearby rest energy rather than create food or medical cures. Markets shorten actual cargo shipment time without changing cargo amounts.

Machines are idle without living aboard crew. Boarding and crew IDs persist and are checked for adult age, kingdom membership, duplicate assignment and conflicting voyages. Boat catches enter cargo first and become kingdom food only at harbor. Crews survive ordinary water/air cleanup when their vessel remains valid; machine loss and crew death remove active assignments. This is one operator per machine, not a multi-seat passenger or rescue simulation.
