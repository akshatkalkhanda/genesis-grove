# Architecture

The game renderer and simulation use web standards, with no runtime library dependencies. Electron provides a desktop window around the same HTML entry point. Its renderer has sandboxing and context isolation enabled, Node.js integration disabled, remote connections blocked by CSP, and external navigation disabled. See Electron's [sandbox documentation](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/tutorial/sandbox).

- `src/simulation.js`: deterministic 192 × 128 tile world, seeded terrain and ore deposits, finite town claims, grouped land-army orders, civilian jobs, trained soldiers, resource-gated technology, mining and equipment crafting, caravan and ship trade and settler voyages, sieges, ecology, brush operations and versioned save validation. No DOM dependencies; tests run in Node.
- `src/renderer.js`: cached procedural pixel terrain, animated creatures, settlement buildings, power effects, camera and minimap.
- `src/app.js`: start menu, deterministic map preview and world setup, input, fixed 0.125-second simulation steps, UI, modal flows, manual device save/export/import, synthesized audio.
- `src/desktop.cjs`: sandboxed Electron window and native application menu.
- `scripts/serve.mjs`: loopback-only development server with a narrow static file allowlist.
- `scripts/smoke.cjs`: isolated Electron session, native pointer actions, save/load and export checks, screenshot capture. Test files live in ignored `artifacts/`.

The app opens on a main menu over an empty, paused world. World setup previews terrain from the same seed and landscape used for the playable world. Creation leaves the world paused until the player starts time or chooses to paint first.

World time is advanced by the fixed-step loop, independently of display refresh rate. Twenty simulated seconds make one sandbox year; challenge years take two simulated seconds. At 20×, a challenge year lasts 0.1 real seconds before rendering overhead. Villager lifetimes use simulation seconds rather than realistic calendar ages. Population caps are 600 people in the sandbox or 1,200 in the challenge, 350 animals, 120 kingdoms, 240 additional towns, 40 settler voyages, and 60 concurrent trade shipments. Caching terrain avoids redrawing thousands of decorated tiles every frame; dynamic creatures, fire and overlays draw separately. Saves preserve RNG state so restored worlds evolve identically with matching future input and steps. Cosmetic power effects are deliberately transient.

Terrain and imported saves are fixed to this world size. No original game's data is loaded. Town claims are derived from surviving settlements and their houses; distant land remains unclaimed, and a captured town changes its claim. Hills and mountains hold copper, tin, iron, and coal. Miners exhaust deposits near towns; research pauses at each age milestone until the required ore is available. Smiths spend ore and wood to equip individual people, reserving materials needed for the next age. Farmers, woodcutters, and builders improve food, timber, and housing. Barracks recruit soldiers who consume more food; only soldiers attack enemies or count toward sieges. Soldiers from the same home share the nearest reachable enemy town on their landmass and move together. Peaceful neighbours move actual surplus goods by caravan or ship, which are lost from the sender until delivery. Individuals move locally, avoiding impassable terrain. Colonist voyages interpolate between settlements; there is no global land pathfinder or naval combat.

Crystal wilds also hold ore. Research can recover knowledge already reached by a living or fallen kingdom, and rebel kingdoms inherit their parent's technology. Newly discovered ages still require materials, while all forging consumes materials.

Biome tile IDs are appended after the original nine terrain types, preserving old saves. New worlds use seeded climate regions; Prismatic Wildlands places eight peoples near preferred habitats. All eleven peoples and eighteen wildlife species use procedural pixel silhouettes. Biomes affect food, timber, fire spread, and ore availability; species affect movement, reproduction, combat, and gathering. Individual inspection selects the nearest living unit and reads its actual simulation values, with no external AI service.

Potential next steps: player-issued army orders, roads and terrain-aware routes, troop movement across water, an economy tied to claimed land, deeper resource chains, creature traits, undo history, autosave slots, and larger-world chunking. These need gameplay and performance testing before expanding current caps.
